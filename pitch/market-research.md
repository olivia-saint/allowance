## 2. Competitors and adjacent players

Facts are only what the sources say (source in brackets). The "how Allowance differs" column is our positioning, not a claim from the source.

| Player | What they do (per source) | How Allowance differs |
|---|---|---|
| **Visa Intelligent Commerce** | Launched 2025. Tokenised "AI-ready" card credentials for agents, with preset dollar limits, merchant-category restrictions and real-time approval prompts; delivered through issuing partners; Trusted Agent Protocol (Oct 2025) for merchants. No native stablecoin support in the agent framework yet. [Crossmint; visa.com/en-us/solutions/intelligent-commerce] | Network infrastructure that needs an issuer/platform partner on top. Allowance needs no bank or issuer: the cap is enforced by the open SPL Token program on Solana, USDC-native, and works today for pay-per-request (x402) APIs. |
| **Mastercard Agent Pay** | Agentic Payments Program (Apr 2025) with "Agentic Tokens"; Agent Pay Acceptance Framework; all US Mastercard cardholders enabled by Nov 2025; partnerships with PayPal, OpenAI, Fiserv; Sept 2026 expansion with "trust and intelligence services". [Crossmint; mastercard.com press release Sept 2026, https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/news-and-trends/press/2026/september/new-trust-and-intelligence-services.html (headline only)] | Same layer as Visa: consumer-card, bank-issued, card-rail. Allowance is the business's own treasury with on-chain caps, aimed at businesses running AI employees, settling in USDC. |
| **Stripe** (Issuing + Agent Toolkit, Shared Payment Tokens, Agentic Commerce Suite) | Programmatic single-use virtual cards for agents via Stripe Issuing / Agent Toolkit; Shared Payment Tokens that can be scoped to a business, time-limited, amount-capped and revoked; co-developed ACP with OpenAI; x402 integration for USDC on Base, primarily fiat-focused. [Crossmint] | Limits are enforced by Stripe's platform; ours are enforced by the chain and auditable by anyone. Allowance is Solana-native and doesn't depend on the merchant being on Stripe. |
| **Ramp Agent Cards** | Virtual cards for AI agents spending on behalf of businesses, built on Visa Intelligent Commerce, with spend limits, merchant controls, transaction visibility; includes agents that fill card details into vendor portals. Fiat only; only for Ramp's own customers. [Crossmint] | Closest in spirit (business spend for agents). Ramp is a closed, US corporate-card product; Allowance is open (any agent framework, any Solana app can verify), stablecoin-native and aimed at SMEs via Saint. Card form-filling is exactly the step we see failing. |
| **Crossmint** | Agent wallets, virtual cards from Visa/Mastercard with scoped permissions and merchant whitelisting, stablecoin onramps in 150+ countries, USDC on 50+ chains, one API for agent platforms. [Crossmint's own page] | Broad multi-chain infrastructure for platforms. Allowance is deliberately narrow: a company card for AI employees where the business keeps custody and the cap is an on-chain SPL delegate allowance. |
| **Slash** | Agent-native business banking and cards via MCP: create cards, set spending limits, ACH, invoices, stablecoin on/off ramps. Only for Slash's own banking customers. [Crossmint] | Requires moving your banking to Slash. Allowance plugs into a business's existing USDC on Solana. |
| **Coinbase x402** | Open payment protocol on HTTP 402, developed by the Coinbase Developer Platform team; supported by Cloudflare, Google and Vercel per Solana. [solana.com] | Complementary, not a competitor: x402 is how an agent pays an API; Allowance is the budget it pays from. Our MVP pays x402-style endpoints from a capped agent card. |

Not covered by sources read this round (mention only generically, if at all): Skyfire (named by Crossmint only as a Visa pilot partner), Payman, Lithic, Coinbase AgentKit. Don't state facts about them in the pitch without checking their sites.

**Honest weaknesses vs incumbents:** card networks have near-universal merchant acceptance; USDC acceptance is much narrower. Our answer is (a) x402/API payments where USDC already works, and (b) a planned card off-ramp for card-only merchants. Big players are already shipping agent spending limits, so our wedge must be custody + on-chain enforcement + SME distribution through Saint, not "limits" alone.
